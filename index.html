Overview

Ergodic Literature

A form of literature that requires non-trivial effort from the reader to traverse the text. Readers actively participate rather than simply consuming information linearly.

Speaker note

Discuss how ergodic literature changes the role of the reader from observer to participant.

Definition

The concept was introduced by Espen Aarseth in 1997 in Cybertext: Perspectives on Ergodic Literature.

  • Ergon = Work
  • Hodos = Path
  • “Work Path”

Key Characteristics

  • Reader participation is necessary.
  • Multiple reading paths.
  • Non-linear structure.
  • Interactive or decision-based progression.
  • May include digital or physical navigation challenges.

Speaker note

Compare a traditional novel to a choose-your-own-adventure experience.

Examples

  • House of Leaves — Mark Z. Danielewski
  • Hopscotch — Julio Cortázar
  • Choose Your Own Adventure books
  • Interactive fiction
  • Hypertext narratives
  • Narrative-driven video games

Conclusion

Ergodic literature expands storytelling beyond traditional reading. The reader becomes an active participant whose choices, navigation, and effort shape the experience.

Key takeaway

Meaning emerges not only from the text itself but from the reader’s interaction with it.
